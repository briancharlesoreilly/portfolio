An invitation
to look closer.

ICHIBAN’s pursuit of simplicity sparked a question: how could the same clarity carry through the way we explain, assemble and make a motorcycle?

This independent portfolio study explores that question through a schematic 3D assembly and a concept motor end cover. The white models and ink outlines borrow the clarity of architectural assembly diagrams.

What is known. What is imagined.

ICHIBAN describes a Kanso-inspired electric motorcycle and a dual-motor system. No internal CAD, motor placement or manufacturing drawings were provided. The geometry, central motor arrangement, assembly order, aluminum component and machining sequence here are conceptual interpretations, not ICHIBAN engineering or an official assembly manual.

The CNC study uses two three-axis setups. Each operation shows a staged geometry snapshot; playback animates the tool, not material removal. These are representative paths, not exported Mastercam paths or machine-ready code. Production would require an engineered drawing, fixture and collision verification, and process validation on a suitable metal-cutting machine.
